When Your System Fails in Phoenix

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer prompt, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll reach a real person who gets a technician dispatched to your property quickly. Arrival in the area is typically within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is exactly the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The urgent calls we run tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, plugged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up on the first trip, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two situations call for quick action from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Phoenix

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
