Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Scottsdale, AZ

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Scottsdale, AZ

As the hottest days approach here in Scottsdale, AZ, our air conditioners become critical components of our home comfort. They work tirelessly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners will eventually wear down and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a major repair or replacement might be on the horizon. Catching these indicators promptly can save you from a sudden and uncomfortable breakdown on the hottest day of the year, and potentially avoid more extensive problems in the long run. Here at Scottsdale HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Is Your Home Not as Cool as It Used to Be?

One of the most noticeable and irritating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a noticeable decrease in its cooling capability. You may constantly be tweaking the thermostat down only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. This could stem from various underlying issues. It could be a refrigerant leak, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. The refrigerant is what makes cooling possible. Another common culprit is a obstructed air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Evaporator coils caked with grime can hinder performance, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. Even a failing compressor, the heart of your AC, could be the cause. If you're experiencing poor temperature control, it's time to have a professional from Scottsdale HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: Listening to Your AC for Clues

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing new and alarming sounds coming from your unit, it's a strong signal that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, critical for smooth operation, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Clicking sounds, especially persistent ones, could indicate a failing relay or a problem with the electrical components. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a major mechanical failure within the compressor or fan. Ignoring these noises can lead to more extensive damage. Timely attention to these sounds can avert a minor problem from escalating into an expensive fix.

Increased Energy Bills: Why Is My Electric Bill So High?

One of the most insidious signs of a failing air conditioner is a creeping increase in your energy bills. When your energy consumption hasn't changed but your bills are spiking, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Several issues can lead to this lack of efficiency. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. If your ducts are leaking, you're cooling your attic instead of your home, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An old or undersized unit might just be inefficient by design. A lack of proper refrigerant makes the system strain. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having Scottsdale HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Good airflow is crucial for effective cooling and even temperature distribution throughout your home. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have significantly less airflow than others, it's a strong indication of an underlying problem. Poor airflow can be attributed to multiple factors. A primary offender is a filter choked with dust and debris, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Problems with the fan motor, separate from the filter, can also cause weak air delivery. If the fan motor is weakening, it won't be able to push air through the ductwork effectively. Obstructions in the ductwork, such as debris or even collapsed sections, can also be a factor. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Fixing poor airflow is vital not only for your comfort but also to prevent overworking your AC and reducing its longevity.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Unwanted Wetness Near Your System?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a unmistakable indicator that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. As your AC cools the air, it removes humidity, and this moisture collects in a drain pan and flows out through a condensate line. Algae, dirt, or debris can block this line, causing water to overflow the pan. Water damage and electrical hazards are possible consequences. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. In more severe cases, a refrigerant leak can also cause ice to form on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overflows the drain pan. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's crucial to take immediate action to prevent further damage. Scottsdale HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

An AC unit should operate in steady cycles, cooling your home to the set temperature before turning off. If you notice your AC unit turning on and off much more frequently than usual, this is known as "short cycling" and it's a significant warning sign. When an AC short cycles, it fails to finish a complete cooling cycle, leading to inefficiency and system strain. Multiple factors can contribute to short cycling. An improperly sized unit (too large for your home) can sometimes short cycle because it cools the space too quickly and then shuts off before it can properly dehumidify. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. Electrical problems or a failing compressor can also cause short cycling. Whatever the reason, frequent short cycling accelerates wear on AC components, resulting in earlier breakdowns and higher energy use. If your AC is short cycling, it's not operating efficiently or effectively, and it needs professional attention.

Old Age: Considering the Age of Your System

Even if your air conditioner isn't exhibiting any dramatic symptoms, its age is a crucial factor in predicting its eventual failure. Typically, central AC units last 10-15 years, with exceptional cases reaching 20 with good maintenance. Once your unit hits or passes this age, it becomes more susceptible to failure and loses efficiency. Many older ACs use R-22, an obsolete refrigerant that's expensive to service. Years of operation mean components in older systems are simply more fatigued. While regular maintenance can certainly extend the life of your AC, there comes a point where repairs become more frequent and costly than investing in a new, more efficient system. If your AC unit is over 10 years old and you're noticing any of the other signs mentioned above, it's a good idea to start planning for a replacement. A modern, efficient model can provide substantial energy savings and enhanced comfort for many years.
